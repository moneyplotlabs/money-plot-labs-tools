Building surface…

Height is years to FI. A step or shelf marks every 5 years, starting exactly on its line. Raised lines mark your own income and expenses (taller) and each grid tick. The flat top is the cap: at or beyond 30 years, including spending levels that never reach FI. Every contour is a straight line, and they all fan out from one point. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom. Filament colors show your print as planned: each color change is a whole layer, so it shows as a horizontal band (3D Print Settings → Colors). By height shades the surface by years instead, which is easier for reading values off it.

Drag either slider and watch the Timeline: the FI date moves instantly.

Future $ (nominal) applies only to the Timeline. All inputs, the map and the 3D model stay in today's dollars, so the FI number grows with inflation on the chart while the FI date stays the same.

FI number = expenses ÷ withdrawal rate.
Blank fields fit themselves to your income and expenses (shown as placeholders). Type a value to fix it; clear it to go back to auto.
3D Print Settings
Nozzle sets the default text sizes and line widths so every feature prints cleanly; orders are printed with a 0.6 mm nozzle. For PrusaSlicer, use Arachne perimeters and 0.2 mm layers. The same scale on both axes (the default) puts a dollar of income and a dollar of spending the same distance apart, so the slopes compare fairly. Match layer heights to your slicer: each iso-year line prints level with its top on a layer boundary, and the base, plot step and text depth are whole layers. Set layer height to 0 to turn this off. The bed defaults to a Prusa MK3S+ (250 × 210 × 210 mm). The preview is a quick draft; the STL is built at its own resolution when you download it, with letter outlines traced inside each grid cell.
A single-nozzle printer can only change color between layers, and here height is years. So a swap at a contour's height colors exactly along that iso-year line. Every contour shape starts at its line and sits uphill of it. A raised step is a cliff up from the line to a level top, filled to the slope: you can feel it, and it reads in one color. A cut shelf is a level notch at exactly the line's height. Its floor takes the accent as an even 1.2 mm line. None leaves the surface alone: with alternating bands, the color change itself is the line. Auto picks step for one color, shelf for accent lines, and none for bands. In PrusaSlicer, drag the layer slider to each Z and click + to add a color change. Labels on the surface sit at many heights, so they print in whatever color their layer has.
Leave a title empty to drop it. The ledges grow or shrink to fit the text. Each line's label goes on its flattest free stretch; a contour with no room is labeled on the back or right ledge where it leaves the plot. Text is cut with straight walls and a flat floor, so letters stack cleanly layer by layer. Fine resolution gives smoother letter edges. Print flat, no supports.

What this does: for every combination of income and expenses in the window, it computes how many years until your portfolio reaches your FI number (expenses ÷ withdrawal rate). Each year the portfolio earns the real return and you add what you save (income − expenses). The result is a surface you can explore, and print.

Method: the years have a closed form, n = ln(1 + R·gap/u) / ln(1+R), where gap is how far you are from your FI number and u is the portfolio's first-year growth. Below the n = 0 line you are already FI. Beyond the line where spending eats all growth (expenses = income + R × portfolio) you never get there. In between, every iso-year line is straight, and all of them pass through one focal point. So a dollar of spending is always worth more than a dollar of income. It cuts savings the same way, and it also raises the FI number by 1/withdrawal-rate dollars.

Note: an illustrative model, not financial advice. It assumes a constant real return, steady income and spending, and no taxes on withdrawals. The stress tester (tool 3) shows how market volatility changes the answer.
